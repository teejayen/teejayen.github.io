I keep hearing two things from trades business owners about AI. Either "it's not relevant to my business" or "I tried ChatGPT once and it was useless."

Both are wrong -- but understandably so. Most AI content is written for office workers. The examples are always email drafting and meeting summaries. If you spend your day on tools, on site, in a van between jobs -- it's easy to conclude this isn't for you.

It is. But not in the ways most people think.

Here are the five highest-value AI applications for trades businesses, what they actually replace, and an honest assessment of what AI is genuinely bad at in your industry.

## 1. Same-day quoting from voice memos

**What it replaces:** The 45 minutes at the kitchen table after dinner, writing up the quote you should have sent three days ago.

**How it works:** You finish a site visit. Driving to the next job, you record a voice memo: "Bathroom reno at 14 Boundary Road. Original copper pipes, needs full re-pipe in PEX. Hot water system is shot -- recommend 315L heat pump. Tempering valve non-compliant, needs replacing. Customer also mentioned dripping kitchen tap, separate job. Access is good, single storey, no asbestos identified."

Upload that recording to any AI tool. Ask it to structure the notes into a scope of works with estimated materials. Then ask it to draft the quote email.

You now have a professional quote in the customer's inbox before you've reached your next job. Your competitor? Still planning to "get to it tonight."

**Setup time:** 10 minutes to learn the workflow.

**Realistic weekly saving:** 2-4 hours, depending on quote volume.

**The competitive edge:** The tradie who quotes same-day wins more jobs. Not because the quote is better -- because it's first. Research consistently shows that speed of response correlates directly with win rate. When a homeowner requests three quotes, the first one back often gets the job.

## 2. SWMS and compliance document generation

**What it replaces:** Copying from a template you last updated in 2019 and hoping it still covers current requirements.

**How it works:** Describe the job: "Solar panel installation, two-storey residential property, tile roof, Queensland. Two workers on site. Working at heights, electrical work, manual handling of panels." Ask AI to generate a site-specific Safe Work Method Statement with hazard identification, risk ratings, control measures, PPE requirements, and emergency procedures.

The output references relevant Australian Standards and covers hazards specific to the job you described -- not generic boilerplate.

**The critical caveat:** This is not a finished document. You still review it, adjust for site-specific conditions, and sign off. AI gets you 80% of the way there. Your trade knowledge and site assessment handle the final 20%. But that 80% used to take an hour of copying, pasting, and adapting. Now it takes 60 seconds.

**Setup time:** 15 minutes to develop a prompt that works for your most common job types.

**Realistic weekly saving:** 1-2 hours, more during tender season.

## 3. Voice-to-structured-job-notes

**What it replaces:** The notes you meant to write up but didn't, the details that fell through the cracks, and the parts order you forgot about until the next morning.

**How it works:** After finishing a job or inspection, you talk into your phone for 45 seconds. Stream of consciousness -- everything you noticed, everything that needs following up, parts to order, issues to raise with the builder. Upload the recording. AI transcribes it, structures it as a site diary entry, separates action items from observations, and flags anything that needs follow-up.

**Why this matters:** Trades businesses lose money on forgotten details. The follow-up job you didn't quote because you forgot the customer mentioned a dripping tap. The parts order that slipped through because the note was on a scrap of paper. The defect you noticed but didn't document, which becomes a dispute six months later.

A 45-second voice memo, structured by AI, catches all of it.

**Setup time:** 5 minutes.

**Realistic weekly saving:** 30-60 minutes of write-up time, plus the value of details you'd otherwise lose.

## 4. Customer follow-up and review requests

**What it replaces:** The follow-up email you know you should send but never get around to.

**How it works:** After completing a job, you give AI the details: "Completed a bathroom renovation for the Smiths. New vanity, floor-to-ceiling tiles, frameless shower screen, heated towel rail. Finished Friday." AI drafts a follow-up email with care instructions for the new fixtures, warranty information, and a polite request for a Google review.

For ongoing maintenance customers, AI can draft seasonal check-in messages: "Hi Dave, it's been 12 months since we serviced your split system. Brisbane summers are getting harder on units -- worth a check before it gets to 40 degrees."

**Why this matters:** Google reviews drive leads. But asking for reviews feels awkward, so most tradies don't do it consistently. A well-worded follow-up email that naturally includes a review request -- sent the day after job completion -- gets reviews without the discomfort.

**Setup time:** 15 minutes to draft template prompts for your most common job types.

**Realistic weekly saving:** 30 minutes, plus the long-term value of consistent reviews and repeat business.

## 5. Apprentice training material capture

**What it replaces:** The knowledge that lives in your head and walks out the door when you're not on site.

**How it works:** Record yourself explaining how to do something -- diagnosing a fault, running cable in an older house, reading a set of plans, identifying non-compliant work. Upload the recording. AI structures it into a step-by-step procedure with safety notes, common mistakes, and decision points.

Over time, you build a library of "how we do things" documentation. When the apprentice asks the same question for the fifth time, you hand them the document.

**Why this matters:** Most trade knowledge is oral. It transfers slowly, inconsistently, and only when the experienced person is available. AI-structured documentation doesn't replace on-the-job training -- but it supplements it. The apprentice can review the procedure before the job, ask better questions on site, and refer back to it later.

**Setup time:** 15-30 minutes per procedure. Build the library over time.

**Realistic value:** Hard to quantify in hours, but significant in reduced rework, fewer mistakes, and faster apprentice development.

## Where AI is a waste of time in trades

Being honest about limitations matters more than overselling.

**Engineering calculations.** Don't use AI to calculate load ratings, cable sizes, pipe sizing for complex systems, or anything where being wrong could hurt someone. AI will confidently give you numbers that look plausible but aren't verified against current Australian Standards. This is the domain of qualified engineers, not language models.

**Site-specific judgement.** AI can't assess the condition of a switchboard by looking at a photo. It can't tell you whether that crack in the wall is structural or cosmetic. It can't read the room when a customer is hesitant about price. Your 15 years of experience at reading sites, reading people, and reading situations is irreplaceable.

**Anything where mistakes are dangerous.** If the task involves safety -- live electrical work, gas fitting, working at heights, confined spaces -- AI is not your decision-making tool. It can help you document the safety plan, but it cannot make safety judgements.

**Complex project management.** For a simple residential job, AI is brilliant at handling the admin. For a multi-stage commercial project with dependencies, subcontractors, and shifting timelines -- you need project management skills and software, not a chatbot.

## The honest cost-benefit

A ChatGPT Plus or Claude Pro subscription costs $30-33 per month. If you save 20 minutes a day -- one quote drafted faster, one follow-up email you wouldn't have sent, one set of job notes captured instead of forgotten -- the subscription pays for itself in the first week.

Most tradies I've spoken to save 1-2 hours per day once they've built the habit. At a conservative value of $80/hour for a qualified tradie's time, that's $400-800 per week of productive time recovered.

The real return isn't just the time saved. It's the quotes that go out same-day instead of three days late. The reviews that come in consistently instead of sporadically. The job notes that capture everything instead of the 60% you remembered to write down.

AI doesn't replace a single trade skill. It handles the paperwork so you can stay on the tools.
