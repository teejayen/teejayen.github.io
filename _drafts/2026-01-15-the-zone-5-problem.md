I've been running for a few months now. "Running" in quotes, because a lot of it is walking. But I'm out there, moving, covering 5km when eight months ago I wouldn't have done it voluntarily.

Here's the problem: my heart rate is stuck in Zone 5.

For anyone unfamiliar with heart rate zones - Zone 5 is the red zone. Maximum effort. The place you're supposed to visit briefly during sprints, not live in for 40 minutes.

Zone 2 is where the magic supposedly happens. Low intensity, conversational pace, building your aerobic base. Every running resource says the same thing: spend 80% of your time in Zone 2. Build the engine before you push it.

I understand this intellectually. The problem is my body hasn't got the memo.

I'll start at what feels like a gentle jog. Within two minutes, heart rate climbs past 150. By five minutes I'm at 170+. Zone 5. I slow down. Still Zone 5. I slow down more - now I'm basically shuffling. Still Zone 5.

The only way to get my heart rate down is to walk.

So I walk. Then jog. Then walk again. The Garmin shows a heart rate graph that looks like a saw blade.

---

At first this felt like failure. Everyone talks about "easy runs" and I can't run easy. My easy is apparently everyone else's sprint effort.

Then I reframed it.

This is just where I am. Untrained cardiovascular system meeting reality. My aerobic base is small because I haven't built it yet. The Zone 5 problem isn't a bug - it's data. It's telling me exactly what needs work.

BJJ taught me this. You don't get annoyed that a white belt can't execute techniques perfectly. They're a white belt. The skill gap is the whole point - that's what training is for.

I'm a white belt runner with a white belt cardiovascular system. Getting frustrated about Zone 5 is like getting frustrated that I can't hold a perfect closed guard after two weeks on the mats.

---

The fix is boring: more Zone 2 work, even if that means walking most of it. Building the base before worrying about pace.

There's no hack here. No shortcut. Just consistent time at low intensity until my heart learns that jogging isn't a near-death experience.

I'm told it takes months. Maybe a year. The aerobic base builds slowly, invisibly, then one day you notice you can jog without your heart rate spiking immediately.

For now, I walk. I jog until Zone 5. I walk again.

It's not impressive. But it compounds.
