Every business document -- a quote, a proposal, a contract, a grant application, a listing presentation, a patient communication -- gets reviewed by one person before it goes out. Usually the person who wrote it.

That's the problem. One person catches one set of issues. The issues they're wired to see. The accountant catches the financial gaps but misses the confusing language. The sales rep catches the competitive weakness but misses the compliance risk. The operations manager catches the delivery risk but misses the customer experience problem.

You can't hire seven reviewers. But you can ask AI to review from seven different angles in about five minutes.

## The seven perspectives

| Perspective | What they look for |
|-------------|-------------------|
| **The Customer** | Does this make sense to me? Would I say yes? What questions would I have? What's confusing? |
| **The New Employee** | Are the instructions clear? What assumptions are being made? Could someone with zero context follow this? |
| **The Accountant** | Do the numbers add up? What are the financial risks? What hasn't been costed? Where's the margin exposure? |
| **The Competitor** | Where are the weaknesses? How would I undercut this? What would I do differently? |
| **The Compliance Officer** | Any regulatory, legal, or safety concerns? Would this pass an audit? Are we meeting our obligations? |
| **The Operations Manager** | Can we actually deliver this? What's the execution risk? Where will this create problems in the workflow? |
| **The Pessimist** | What could go wrong? What assumptions are we making? What are we not seeing? What happens if this fails? |

You don't need all seven every time. Start with the two or three that matter most for the document in front of you.

## How it works

Open any AI tool -- ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot. Paste your document. Then:

> "Read this [quote / proposal / contract / application] as if you are the customer receiving it. What questions would you have? What's confusing? What would make you hesitate?"

Read the feedback. Then:

> "Now read the same document as an accountant. What are the financial risks? Does the maths add up? What hasn't been costed?"

Different perspective, different issues. Every time.

The technique works because each perspective has a different set of priorities, fears, and evaluation criteria. The customer cares about clarity and value. The accountant cares about numbers and risk. The competitor looks for weaknesses. The pessimist stress-tests assumptions. A single-perspective review always has blind spots. Seven perspectives cover most of them.

## Worked example: a trades quote

A Brisbane electrician sends out a quote for a switchboard upgrade at a residential property. Before sending it, he runs it through three perspectives.

**The Customer perspective** flags:
- The exclusions section is buried at the bottom and easy to miss -- the customer might assume everything is included
- There's no explanation of *why* the switchboard needs upgrading -- the customer doesn't understand the safety imperative
- The payment terms say "50% deposit" but don't explain what happens if additional work is discovered
- The quote uses abbreviations (RCD, RCBO) without explanation

**The Accountant perspective** flags:
- No allowance for make-good of the meter panel if it needs moving
- Labour is quoted as a lump sum with no indication of hours -- if the job runs long, there's no mechanism for additional charges
- No mention of tip fees for disposal of old board and materials
- GST treatment is ambiguous

**The Competitor perspective** flags:
- No warranty information -- a competitor who includes a 12-month workmanship guarantee wins on trust
- No timeline commitment -- a competitor who promises "completed within 5 business days of deposit" wins on certainty
- No mention of the electrician's specific qualifications or experience -- the customer has no reason to choose this quote over the cheaper one

Three reviews, five minutes. The electrician adjusts the quote: moves exclusions to a prominent section, adds a plain-language explanation of why the upgrade matters, includes warranty terms, commits to a timeline, and adds a one-line credential.

None of these issues were about price. They were about clarity, trust, and professionalism. The kind of things that determine which of the three quotes the customer says yes to.

## Worked example: a grant application

A community services not-for-profit is applying for $50,000 in funding for a youth mentoring program. The program manager drafts the application and runs it through three perspectives.

**The Funder perspective** flags:
- The application leads with the organisation's history rather than the community need -- funders care about impact, not institutional biography
- Outcome measures are vague ("improve wellbeing") with no defined metrics or baseline data
- The budget doesn't clearly link expenditure to program activities -- it's not obvious what the money will actually fund
- No mention of how the program will be sustained after the funding period ends

**The Pessimist perspective** flags:
- The application assumes 100% participant retention -- what happens if 30% drop out?
- Volunteer mentors are the backbone of delivery, but there's no contingency for volunteer attrition
- The timeline assumes council approval for the venue by March -- what's the fallback if that's delayed?
- No mention of safeguarding procedures, which most funders now require explicitly

**The Compliance Officer perspective** flags:
- Working With Children checks are mentioned but the policy isn't referenced
- Data collection for outcome measurement may require ethics approval depending on the funder's requirements
- The partnership letters from two organisations are dated 18 months ago -- funders may question whether the partnerships are still active

The program manager rewrites the opening to lead with community need and impact data, adds specific outcome metrics with baselines, builds in a retention buffer, updates the partnership letters, and explicitly addresses safeguarding. The application goes from average to competitive.

## Adapting for your industry

The seven perspectives are universal, but you can swap in more targeted angles depending on your sector:

| Your industry | Most useful starting perspectives |
|---------------|----------------------------------|
| **Trades** | The Customer + The Accountant (catches unclear scope and financial gaps) |
| **Professional services** | The Client + The Compliance Officer (catches confusing language and regulatory gaps) |
| **Retail and hospitality** | The Customer (as Google Reviewer) + The Kitchen Hand (catches experience gaps and operational blind spots) |
| **Health and wellness** | The Patient + The AHPRA Auditor (catches clinical communication issues and regulatory gaps) |
| **Real estate** | The Buyer + The Competing Agent (catches listing weaknesses and competitive exposure) |
| **Transport and logistics** | The Freight Customer + The NHVR Auditor (catches service gaps and compliance issues) |
| **Not-for-profits** | The Funder + The Pessimist (catches weak impact framing and unaddressed risks) |

## The meta-point

The Seven Perspectives technique isn't about making AI smarter. It's about systematising the review process that experienced professionals already do intuitively -- but usually only from one or two angles, and usually under time pressure.

A partner at an accounting firm naturally reviews a document from the compliance angle. But they might not think to review it from the client's perspective -- how confusing is the language? What questions will the client have? A trades business owner naturally reviews their quote from a pricing angle. But they might not think about how a competitor's quote stacks up side by side.

The seven perspectives aren't a replacement for expertise. They're a way to apply your expertise from angles you wouldn't normally consider, in the five minutes before you hit send.

Every document you send is a representation of your business. Running it through three perspectives before it goes out is five minutes that protects your reputation, catches your blind spots, and consistently improves the quality of everything that leaves your desk.

Pick a document sitting on your desk right now. Run it through two perspectives. See what surfaces.
