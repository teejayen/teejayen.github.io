I've added disclosure markers to posts on this blog.

You'll now see a small note below the date on some posts: "Written with AI" or "Assisted by AI". Each links to [a page explaining what that means](/ai/).

The short version: I use Claude extensively for writing. Not as a content generator - as a thinking partner that happens to be better at the mechanics of prose than I am.

## Why disclose

One reason I maintain this blog is ownership. Markdown files, no algorithm, no platform deciding who sees what. Direct access to my thinking.

That transparency should extend to how the thinking gets expressed.

We're at an interesting moment. The tools exist. They're good. Hiding their use feels dishonest. If I can output more than I input while staying true to my voice and beliefs, I'll keep using them. The disclosure is respect for you as a reader - you can weight things accordingly.

## How it works

I've trained Claude on years of my writing. Blog posts, LinkedIn updates, emails, comments. When it produces something, it sounds like me because I've shaped it to.

The process: I provide significant context - the problem, the angle, what I don't want to say. We iterate. I edit before anything goes live. The ideas are mine. The judgment is mine. The tool handles mechanics.

## The spectrum

Not every post is the same:

**Written with AI** - Claude did most of the writing based on my direction. I provided the thinking; AI handled expression.

**Assisted by AI** - I wrote the core. Claude helped with structure or polish as a final pass.

**No marker** - Unaided, or predates the AI workflow.

## The honest part

"I wrote this" gets complicated when the keystrokes came from a model trained on my previous keystrokes. The thinking happened in conversation. The refinement was collaborative.

I don't have clean language for this. Neither does anyone else yet. The [full philosophy](/ai/) goes deeper if you're interested.

What I know: the ideas are genuine, the voice is trained on mine, the editing is human. Whether that counts as "writing" is a question I'm leaving open.
